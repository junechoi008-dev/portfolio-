Tinder × Boiler Room

The Challenge

The audience was Gen Z women aged 18–24 across EMEA, and for them dating had become emotionally expensive. Swiping reads as performance rather than possibility: 73% report digital exhaustion, 76% are burnt out from dating apps, and 80% feel lonely despite being constantly connected — with financial pressure and doomscrolling habits deepening the fatigue. The brief wasn't a reach problem; it was a fatigue problem.

The insight

They can curate an identity,
but they can't curate connection.

Gen Z copes with the pressure through hyper-curation — controlling how they're perceived. The same instinct for control is what makes authentic connection harder to reach.

The objective

Low-pressure, authentic connection

Position Tinder as a space for real connection rather than another performative platform, and convert that into brand consideration and growth.

MAU target +137K EMEA MAU roughly 1% of the target market
02

Strategy

If control is the barrier, the answer isn't to remove it — it's to give it a setting. Tinder brings the connection, a partner brand creates a controllable, curated environment, and a shared interest supplies the reason to talk. That framing turned the brief from "get more swipes" into "build a room worth walking into."

Music was the natural shared-interest domain — 85% of Gen Z say music defines who they are, 80% say concerts bring people closer, and Tinder matches rise 10% during Music Mode — because live music is low-pressure and high-connection by default. Boiler Room was chosen as the partner for its cultural credibility (500K+ tour attendees, 283M monthly online impressions, a record of breaking artists before the mainstream) and its overlap with Tinder's audience: 18–34, 45% LGBTQIA+, built on self-expression and IRL community.

The big idea

"Step Into the Circle"

Connecting with someone. Letting them into your circle. And stepping into a circle of common ground — the crowd around a DJ booth being the most literal version of it.

Activation · a four-city tour that scales

A DJ-set tour built as a proof-of-concept-to-arena curve, growing from an 800-capacity room to 16,000 across four cities.

Berlin 800 attendees · proof of concept
Bilbao scale-up · second market
Bristol scale-up · third market
Lyon 16,000 attendees · arena finale
In-app mechanics
  • Boiler Room tagging in Tinder's Explore / Interest section
  • DJ-profile lookalike matching, so taste becomes a matching signal
  • Ticketing through a match — users match with a "DJ" persona in-app to unlock subsidised or free tickets
On-site branding
  • Tinder × Boiler Room photo zones built for the feed
  • Goody bags — disposable cameras, dating-preference bands, QR codes to Tinder Gold
  • Every touchpoint routes attendance back into the app after the night ends.
Media & campaign plan
Phase 1 · Teaser

UGC seeding and influencer-led hype, with in-app tagging pulling users together around the shared interest.

Phase 2 · Ticketing

Early-bird ballot access and DJ-matching mechanics to drive app opens and time spent in-app.

Phase 3 · Event

Branded venues, photo zones and goody bags converting attendance into renewed app engagement.

Underneath the phases sat the operational work: influencer tiering across nano, micro and macro, and a full budget and P&L model supporting a three-month, four-city execution timeline.
