The Challenge

The brief was a brand awareness campaign for a flavored milk whose one distinguishing feature is its handle — a bottle you grip rather than sip from. The obstacle was cultural: "cheers" in Korea belongs to adults and alcohol, so borrowing the ritual for teenagers risked reading as forced or, worse, inappropriate. The concept had to reframe the toast so naturally that teens would perform it themselves — on camera, in public.

Objectives

The campaign had to position the product as a sweet, relaxing daily treat for teens and lift brand recognition and positive perception — but awareness alone wasn't the bar. Success meant teens taking part themselves rather than only watching, so participation was treated as the real measure from the start.

02

Strategy

The reframe came from wordplay teens already use: "JJAN" (cheers) → "JJIN" (thick, rich) — you don't toast with alcohol, you toast with milk thick enough to deserve it. On top of that sat a behaviour that already existed on their feeds: filming the clink of two drinks with Instagram's Boomerang. We named it "Jjan-merang", so the campaign wasn't asking teens to learn anything — only to point the trend at our bottle.

Media was built to feed participation rather than just reach. Six short-form videos, including a 6-second bumper, ran as vertical ads on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels; target-relevant influencers demonstrated the toast first so the format felt already-populated; and every ad landed on a microsite carrying the event details, the participation guide and the prizes — a single destination that turned a view into an entry.

Six short-forms, one toast

Six vertical films — including a 6-second bumper — each staged a different teen "cheers" occasion and ended on the handle-clink, the exact gesture we wanted copied. Two of the cuts answered the obvious objection ("what if I can't buy the product?") by showing entries made with a drawing or a phone screen, which is where a good share of the participation came from.
